## Code (part B)
- I traced the stacks of `lfr-arm`, `lfr-handler?`, `lfr-handle`, `lfr-put-back`, `lfr-keep`, `lfr-unsave`, `lf-tac-alternative`/`lfr-alt-mode`, `lfr-pass-or-skip`, `lfr-logs?`, `lfr-sorry-in`, `lfr-push`/`lfr-flagged`/`lfr-moded`, `lfr-take`, and the try / first / tried / last / repeat / any modes. All are correct.
- Releasing saves:
  - The save is taken before the hold and the log entry.
  - It is released at a good end (keep) or at a handled error (put-back).
  - The topmost handler is always the newest save, because inner handlers are found first and `lf-tac-try` restores before rethrowing.
  - On a THROW that is not Lean's, frame saves stay until the next `lfr-fresh` forgets them. That is the same as before the branch. The trail marks of those saves are not committed; I did not check whether a later declaration in the same check could see them.
- Frame fields after a restore:
  - `tfr-held` is cleared.
  - `tfr-todo`, `tfr-acc` and `tfr-at` change only outside a save (acc+ runs after the restore or the commit).
  - A goal unassigned by a restore is seen as open again by `lf-goals-next-open`, as Lean's `isAssigned` check does.
- Frame addresses are re-fetched after put-back. No new module-level state: mode records, `tac-shape-alt` and the 469/490 catalogue entries are written at load only.
- The internal `lf-tac-try` / `lf-tac-first-of` (an xt plus CATCH) and the frame handlers (steps in the loop) share save, restore, commit and the Lean-error test. Their control differs by necessity, so they need not be joined now. A plan row could merge them if macro alternatives ever become step lists.

**1. Fixed (`42a4f002`): a brace that is a have-by's whole body.** Pinned Lean 4.35.0, for
`try (have j : b = a := by X)` / `exact h`, rejects (the error logged) for X = `{ exact h }` and
`{ { exact h } }` and accepts (the error thrown, try takes the have back) for `· exact h`,
`(· exact h)`, `({ exact h })`, `({ { exact h } })`, `skip; { exact h }`, `(· (· exact h))`,
`exact h`, `focus · exact h`, `all_goals exact h` and `· exact h` followed by `exact k`; the
brace form followed by more steps does not parse (`by {` always takes the brace as the whole
sequence). So a brace that is the whole body of the `by` sees Tactic.run's recover, true, and
anything one step deeper sees it false: a split observed on pinned Lean, not found in its source
(the comment above `lfr-recover-in` now says so). LeanForth accepted r13 (`by-have-by: j { { ' h
by-exact } } by-end` under `by-try`), which Lean rejects. Now braces have their own mode
(`lf-mode-brace`, flag `lfr-whole-logs`), and `lfr-whole?` (the brace the first and only step of
a fresh Tactic.run's frame) makes the recover below the brace true in `lfr-below-recover`: its
error is final, 490 with a save open (r13 and `{ ' h by-exact }` under `by-try` or a first
alternative but the last; Lean rejects, and since a later failing step inside the try can take
the message back, as `try (have j : b = a := by { { exact h } }); exact k` shows, LeanForth
cannot answer more), Lean's 96 at the top level. A LeanForth `{ }` stands for both Lean's
`· tacs` and `{ tacs }`; in this one place they differ, and 490 wrongly accepts neither twin.
The brace's own recover stays false, so a brace inside it throws, as Lean's. The throw-path
texts (recover.lf's rec_try_brace, rec_try_brace_fail, rec_brace_try, rec_then_try,
w-try-brace.lf, the unit tests) now put a `by-show` before the brace, with Lean twins
`by show b = a; (· exact h)`; `u-try-have-brace.lf` and `u-try-have-brace2.lf` pin the
whole-body shapes (490; twins in blocks-3-wrong.lean reject). Unit tests: whole-body brace
under try, under a non-last first alternative, at top level (single and double), unsolved goals
at its `}`; the one-deeper shapes (after by-show, followed by a step, inside by-focus, a brace
only in a brace under try). Faults `tb2b3-whole-body-throws`, `tb2b3-whole-not-only`,
`tb2b3-whole-not-first` and `tb2b3-whole-any-block` are caught; `tb2b1-below-own-flag` and

LeanForth on two large bodies of proof: all of Mathlib, and the Navier-Stokes and Euler formalization
====================================================================================================

Both runs below were made on 2026-11-20 with the build tagged svfig-prepre-alpha (Mathlib on the
tagged commit 677800d7; Navier-Stokes on 1b136d73, the same source with two later documentation
commits). In both, LeanForth accepted every declaration.

What LeanForth did here is CHECK proofs. It did not find them. People and Lean wrote and built the
proofs; LeanForth's own kernel then checked every one again, independently of Lean's program.


PART 1. THE PATH OF A PROOF, FROM AN IDEA TO LEANFORTH'S VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------

Steps 1 to 6 are done by people and by Lean. Steps 7 and 8 are LeanForth's.

 1. The idea.
    A mathematician has a claim and an argument for it, on paper. Nothing is checked yet.

 2. The Lean theorem.
    The claim is written as a statement in Lean's language. Every notion in it must already be
    defined in Lean (real numbers, derivatives, "smooth", ...), usually by the library Mathlib.
    Example (the first Navier-Stokes result below):

        theorem navier_stokes_breakdown_R3 (nu : ℝ) (hnu : nu > 0) :
            ∃ (u₀ : ℝ³ → ℝ³) (f : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ³),
            InitialVelocityConditionDecay u₀ ∧ ForceConditionDecay f ∧
            ¬ (∃ v p, NavierStokesExistenceAndSmoothnessRn nu u₀ f v p)

    In words: for every viscosity nu above 0 there are a starting velocity u₀ and a force f, both
    well behaved, for which no smooth solution v, p exists for all time.

 3. The tactic steps.
    The argument is written as a proof script: lines such as  intro ,  rw [...] ,  induction ,
    simp ,  exact ... . Each line is a tactic: an instruction that changes what is left to prove.
    A large proof is thousands of smaller theorems (lemmas), each with its own script.

 4. Elaboration.
    Lean reads statement and script and fills in everything a person leaves out: the types of the
    variables, which "+" is meant (on naturals? on reals?), the hidden arguments. Then it runs the
    tactics. The result is one fully explicit term per theorem: the proof term. A proof term is
    much larger than its script and is not meant to be read.

 5. Lean's proof.
    Lean's kernel, a small program, checks each proof term against its statement by a few fixed
    rules. Only what the kernel accepts counts as proved in Lean. The tactics and the elaborator
    are not trusted: whatever they build, the kernel checks.

 6. The export.
    A tool (lean4export) writes every checked declaration (definitions, theorems, their proof
    terms) into one text file, one JSON record per line: names, universe levels, expressions,
    declarations. This file holds the proofs in the form Lean's kernel saw them.

 7. The import into LeanForth.
    LeanForth, a program written in Forth, reads that file with its own reader and builds its own
    copy of every expression. It uses no part of Lean for this.

 8. LeanForth's kernel.
    LeanForth's own kernel checks every declaration again, by Lean's rules, written independently
    in Forth. For each it prints a verdict: accepted, rejected, not supported yet, or out of a
    resource. A second, different program agreeing with Lean is the point of the exercise.

For small theorems LeanForth now also does steps 2 to 5 itself: a statement and tactic steps written
in LeanForth text are elaborated by LeanForth and checked by its kernel. See the two pages beside
this one, decide-showcase.txt and s2-showcase.txt. The two runs below are steps 7 and 8 only.


PART 2. THE TWO RUNS, WITH THE SIZE AND TIME OF EVERY STEP
-----------------------------------------------------------

Machine for all times: one desktop, 10 cores, 39 GB of memory, Linux under WSL2.

A. "Finite time blowup for Navier-Stokes and Euler equations" (OpenAI)
   Source: https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler , commit f9e8bc5b, built with Lean
   4.34.0-rc2. It is one of the large tests of the Lean Kernel Arena, whose description of it is:
   "OpenAI's Lean formalization of finite-time blowup for the three-dimensional incompressible
   Navier-Stokes equations with smooth forcing (on Euclidean space and on the periodic torus), and
   of unforced Euler blowup."

   The four main theorems, each accepted by LeanForth with Lean's three standard axioms
   (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) and no other:
       NavierStokes.Comparator.navier_stokes_breakdown_R3
       NavierStokes.Comparator.navier_stokes_breakdown_periodic
       Euler.euler_breakdown_R3
       Euler.exists_compact_smooth_euler_singularity
   What these statements mean for mathematics is the authors' claim and is described in their
   papers. LeanForth's verdict says one thing: the proof terms check.

   One declaration is counted "not proved": sorryAx itself, the placeholder axiom that Lean's

   axioms.
   Lean's own kernel was not timed on this file here.

The commands (bin/leanforth is LeanForth's command line):

   bin/leanforth check --no-manifest --any-lean-version --resources --jobs 8 --fuel 250000000 \
       --memory-per-check 8192 navier-stokes-euler.ndjson
   bin/leanforth check --no-manifest --resources --jobs 8 --fuel 250000000 --memory-per-check 8192 \
       --save-snapshot mathlib.lfsnap mathlib.ndjson

   --any-lean-version: the Navier-Stokes file was written by Lean 4.34.0-rc2; LeanForth's verdicts
   are by the rules of Lean 4.35.0 and it says so in a note. --no-manifest: these two files have no
   signed list of contents beside them. Neither file is in the repository (1.6 GB and 6.3 GB);
   docs/user/COMMAND_LINE.md says how to make such exports.

What these runs do not show. LeanForth's kernel has not yet passed its human review, and no outside
expert has audited it. Agreement with Lean on 859,108 declarations is strong evidence that it follows
Lean's rules; it is not a proof that it does.
